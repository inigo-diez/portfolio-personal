# MASTER PROMPT — POCKET OS × INFINITE CANVAS

> Original creative brief as provided on 2026-10-19, kept for reference only. **The working version is [BRIEF.md](BRIEF.md) (v2).**

## ROLE

Act as a world-class creative developer, senior frontend engineer, interaction designer, digital art director, and industrial product designer.

You specialize in creating extraordinary interactive websites that combine refined visual design, functional software, tactile interactions, and memorable digital experiences.

Your task is to completely redesign my personal portfolio.

This must not be another generic AI-generated developer portfolio.

It should feel like an original, carefully art-directed digital product.

**Project concept: POCKET OS × INFINITE CANVAS**

**Creative balance: 80% Pocket OS / 20% Infinite Canvas.**

The Pocket OS is the central object and recognizable visual identity.

The Infinite Canvas is an extension of the Pocket OS, primarily used to explore projects.

The two experiences must feel like parts of a single, cohesive interface.

# 3. INDUSTRIAL DESIGN OF THE POCKET OS

The Pocket OS device is the most important visual component of the entire website.

It must look like a believable, meticulously designed physical object.

## Form factor

Explore a design combining:

- A premium digital MP3 player.
- A vintage PDA.
- A compact futuristic computer.
- A custom experimental electronic device.

The device should be compact, elegant, slightly retro, and unmistakably technological.

Suggested features:

- White or brushed-silver housing.
- Carefully designed rounded rectangular silhouette.
- Premium metallic edge detailing.
- Small but readable display.
- Circular navigation control, tactile directional pad, or hybrid input system.
- Minimal physical buttons.
- Subtle hardware markings.
- Carefully positioned sensors or indicator lights.
- Delicate physical shadows.
- Realistic material separation.
- Restrained surface reflections.

Avoid excessive realism that makes the interface look like a static photograph.

It must feel like an interactive product rendered in a browser.

## Originality

Do not make an exact iPod replica.

Create an original silhouette, proportions, control layout, and brand identity.

For example, explore a device with:

- A screen larger than a traditional iPod screen.
- A simplified navigation wheel.
- A secondary small control.
- A unique device identifier or engraved logo.
- A coherent custom operating system.

Before implementation, present two device silhouette concepts and recommend one.

Prioritize visual harmony and ease of navigation.

# 7. THE SIGNATURE TRANSFORMATION

This transition is the main creative feature of the portfolio.

It must be implemented exceptionally well.

## Trigger

The user navigates to Projects inside the Pocket OS and selects it.

## Animation sequence

1. The selected menu item responds.
2. The display becomes the visual focus.
3. The device subtly shifts or scales.
4. The screen expands into a much larger interface.
5. The new interface grows into a spatial project environment.
6. Project cards become visible.
7. The user gains control of the Infinite Canvas.

The visual connection between the screen and the canvas must be obvious.

Use a shared-element transition, FLIP-style animation, or another suitable technique.

Do not fake the transition with an unrelated page reload or an abrupt replacement.

## Returning

The experience must be reversible.

When the user chooses Home or Back:

1. The canvas returns to its initial visual state.
2. The expanded space contracts.
3. The screen becomes part of the device again.
4. The Pocket OS returns to its original position.

Avoid awkward jumps or resetting the entire page unexpectedly.

## Interaction timing

Transitions should feel polished, not slow.

Prefer short, responsive microinteractions and a carefully timed main transformation.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

# 8. INFINITE CANVAS

The Infinite Canvas is the secondary experience.

It should be impressive but restrained.

Its purpose is to create a memorable and flexible way to browse projects.

## Overall appearance

Use a large, clean white space.

Include a very subtle grid or coordinate system if appropriate.

Distribute project cards across a carefully planned spatial composition.

Use variation in:

- Position.
- Scale.
- Orientation, very sparingly.
- Card size.
- Visual hierarchy.

The canvas should feel spacious and curated.

It should not resemble an uncontrolled brainstorming whiteboard.

## Interaction

Implement:

- Click-and-drag panning.
- Smooth trackpad interaction.
- Controlled zooming.
- Touch dragging.
- Pinch zoom on supported devices.
- Reset-view control.
- Return-to-device control.
- A conventional project-list alternative.

The user should never become lost inside the canvas.

Include subtle controls such as:

Projects / Explore · Zoom + / − · Reset · List View · Home

## Layout strategy

Use a defined starting composition.

When entering the canvas, the user must immediately see several real projects.

Do not place all content outside the initial viewport.

Project positions should be deterministic.

Use appropriate limits or visual guidance to prevent navigation into large empty regions.

A spatial illusion is more important than mathematically infinite dimensions.

## Project cards

Cards should feel like premium editorial objects.

Use real previews, graphics, screenshots, or meaningful project visuals.

Each card should show:

- Project title.
- Short description.
- Relevant technologies.
- A visual preview.
- A clear action to open the project.

Cards can expand, unfold, or move forward when selected.

Avoid generic gradient backgrounds or meaningless stock imagery.

# 12. MOTION AND MICROINTERACTIONS

Motion should communicate a physical or spatial relationship.

Use:

- Spring-inspired controls.
- Controlled device movement.
- Shared-element transitions.
- Refined card unfolding.
- Subtle scale changes.
- Delicate shadow changes.
- Precise navigation feedback.

Avoid:

- Long forced sequences.
- Excessive parallax.
- Constant floating animations.
- Animations on every scroll event.
- Gratuitous cursor effects.

Motion must be responsive and meaningful.

Implement reduced-motion fallbacks.

No important information should require watching an animation.

- Vite.

Use a maintainable structure such as:

- App shell.
- Design tokens.
- Pocket device.
- Device controls.
- Operating system UI.
- Navigation state.
- Transition system.
- Project canvas.
- Project card.
- Project case study.
- Conventional navigation.
- About and Experience.
- Contact.

## State model

- home
- device-menu
- device-section
- canvas-transition

- project-detail

## Canvas architecture

## Routes

Project detail pages must have shareable URLs.

# 15. ACCESSIBILITY AND PERFORMANCE

Treat usability as an essential part of the design.

Requirements:

- Semantic HTML.
- Full keyboard navigation.
- Visible focus states.
- Appropriate accessible names.
- Screen-reader-compatible project navigation.
- Sufficient text contrast.
- Responsive touch targets.
- No mandatory gestures for accessing content.
- Alternative conventional navigation.
- Support for prefers-reduced-motion.
- No forced sound.
- No unnecessary animation during scrolling.

The portfolio must remain usable when the decorative device presentation is unavailable.

Target WCAG 2.2 AA conformance where applicable.

## Performance

Use efficient animation techniques.

Avoid unnecessary 3D rendering.

Lazy-load substantial project assets.

Keep important text available immediately.

Avoid layout shifts.

Optimize images.

Use a static-friendly build unless there is a compelling reason not to.

Treat Lighthouse results as diagnostics and validate real interaction behavior, not only numerical scores.

# 17. IMPLEMENTATION PLAN — SIX PHASES

Do not attempt to implement everything in one uncontrolled coding pass.

## PHASE 1 — Audit and art direction

Inspect: existing repository; current HTML/CSS/JavaScript architecture; existing project content; existing links; available visual assets; current hosting configuration.

Create: a concise content audit; two Pocket OS silhouette proposals; a visual design system; a recommended homepage composition; a conceptual Infinite Canvas layout; desktop and mobile wireframes.

Present the proposed design before proceeding to a complete implementation.

Use genuine visual mockups or browser-rendered prototypes when possible. Do not rely on descriptions alone.

**Completion criteria:** The device concept, layout, palette, and overall experience are visually understandable and ready for approval.

## PHASE 2 — Pocket OS implementation

Build: responsive homepage; device structure; original hardware styling; functional controls; internal OS screen; menu navigation; keyboard and touch support.

**Completion criteria:** The device is visually convincing, interactive, and capable of navigating the main portfolio sections.

## PHASE 3 — Infinite Canvas and transition

Build: the device-to-canvas transition; spatial project layout; pan and zoom controls; project card navigation; reset view; list view; reversible transition back to the device.

**Completion criteria:** Users can enter the canvas, explore projects, open a project, and return without becoming lost or encountering broken navigation.

## PHASE 4 — Real content integration

Migrate and improve: About; Projects; Case studies; Skills; Experience; CV; Contact.

Inspect GitHub repositories to verify project information. Maintain working links. Do not lose substantial technical content during migration.

**Completion criteria:** The site can function as a complete professional portfolio, not merely an interactive concept demo.

## PHASE 5 — Refinement and originality

Improve: typography and spacing; material rendering; button feedback; motion timing; card composition; transition continuity; accessibility; mobile experience.

Implement no more than a few meaningful signature details.

**Completion criteria:** The website looks cohesive, distinctive, and professionally finished.

## PHASE 6 — Validation and deployment

Test: desktop browsers; mobile viewport behavior; keyboard interactions; touch interactions; project navigation; direct URLs; reduced-motion behavior; loading and animation performance; CV and external links; deployment compatibility.

Build and deploy using the repository's appropriate workflow.

Do not overwrite production without a suitable backup or version-control checkpoint.

**Completion criteria:** The portfolio is functional, accessible, maintainable, and deployable.

# 18. IMPORTANT ANTI-GENERIC DESIGN RULES

- Do not use a standard hero-section-plus-cards template.
- Do not replace the device with a decorative illustration beside a heading.
- Do not make the Infinite Canvas the default homepage.
- Do not produce a generic desktop OS clone.
- Do not create fake terminal windows just to communicate technical expertise.
- Do not introduce irrelevant visualizations, random 3D objects, fake achievements, or meaningless dashboard metrics.
- Do not use placeholder text in the final completed portfolio unless a required real detail remains unavailable; identify any such placeholder explicitly.
- Do not claim that unfinished demonstrations are functional.
- Avoid overengineering.
- Make every interaction serve a purpose.

The strongest result is not the one with the most animations or features.

It is the one with the most coherent design.
